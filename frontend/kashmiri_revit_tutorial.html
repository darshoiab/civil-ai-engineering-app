Chapter 01
Introduction to
Kashmiri Architecture
Understanding Traditional Elements Before Modeling

Kashmir's vernacular architecture is one of the most distinctive in the Indian subcontinent, shaped over centuries by the region's severe winters, heavy snowfall, seismic activity, and abundant deodar and walnut forests. Before modeling a Kashmiri house in Revit, it is essential to understand the structural and aesthetic elements that define this style.

1.1 Taq Construction System

The Taq system is a traditional load-bearing stone masonry technique where vertical stone piers carry horizontal deodar timber bands at each floor level. The horizontal bands (called dhajji) provide the seismic flexibility that allows the building to absorb earthquake energy without total collapse.

Taq System — Key Specs

  • Stone masonry piers: 300–450 mm thick
  • Deodar timber bands at each floor level
  • Openings formed between piers
  • Lime mortar jointing
  • Multi-storey: 2–3 floors typical

Dhajji Dewari — Key Specs

  • Braced timber frame (deodar) primary structure
  • Brick or stone infill in small panels
  • Mud mortar or lime used in infill
  • Visible "quilt" pattern on exterior wall
  • Excellent seismic performance

1.3 Climate Considerations

Design Principle Kashmir receives snowfall from November to March. Roofs must be pitched at minimum 30° to ensure snow slides off. Eave overhangs of 600–900 mm protect mud-plastered or brick walls from rain and meltwater. All window sills are set at 900 mm above floor level to retain interior warmth.

Levels & Grids
Establishing the Vertical Framework of Your 30×30 ft Kashmiri Home

Levels define each horizontal plane (floor-to-floor heights) in your building. For a 30×30 ft (9.1×9.1 m) two-storey Kashmiri house, we set up four levels.

3.1 Setting Up Levels

1
Open an Elevation View In the Project Browser, expand Views → Elevations → South Elevation. Double-click to open.
2
Set Level 1 — Ground Floor The default Level 1 is at 0.0 m. This is your ground floor (±0.00 m finished floor level).
3
Create Level 2 — First Floor Architecture tab → Datum → Level. Draw a horizontal line at 3.0 m above Level 1. Name it "Level 2 – First Floor".
4
Create Roof Level Add another level at 6.1 m (3.1 m above Level 2). Name it "Roof Level". This is the top of the wall / eave line.
5
Verify Floor-to-Floor Heights Ground Floor to First Floor: 3.0 m · First Floor to Roof: 3.1 m (extra 100 mm for structural slab)
Level Schedule — 30×30 ft Kashmiri House
Level NameElevationNotes
Level 1 — Ground Floor0.00 m (±0)Entrance, living, kitchen, guest room
Level 2 — First Floor3.00 mBedrooms, Zoon Dub balcony
Roof Level6.10 mEave / top of wall, start of roof pitch
Roof Ridge8.50 m (approx.)Peak of gable — auto-calculated by Revit

3.2 Setting Up Grids

1
Switch to Floor Plan — Level 1 Project Browser → Views → Floor Plans → Level 1.
2
Draw Vertical Grids (A, B, C) Architecture → Datum → Grid. Draw vertical grids at: A = 0.0 m, B = 4.6 m, C = 9.1 m (total 30 ft = 9.144 m ≈ 9.1 m).
3
Draw Horizontal Grids (1, 2, 3) Draw horizontal grids at: 1 = 0.0 m, 2 = 4.6 m, 3 = 9.1 m.

Kashmiri exterior walls use stone or brick masonry (Taq system) with deodar timber bands. In Revit, we create a custom wall type to reflect this.

1

2
Duplicate and Name the Wall Type Click Duplicate. Name it Kashmiri_Ext_Wall_300mm. Click OK.

4
Apply Material — Stone Rubble Masonry For the core layer, click the material cell → Browse → search "Stone" → select Stone, Rubble. Assign color: warm grey (RGB 140, 130, 120).

1

3

Create a second wall type for internal partitions: Kashmiri_Int_Wall_115mm — single-leaf brick (115 mm) with plaster both sides (total 155 mm).

Room Layout — Ground Floor (30×30 ft) Draw partition walls to create: Main Entrance Hall (2.0×1.5 m) · Living Room (4.5×4.0 m) · Dining Room (3.0×3.5 m) · Kitchen (3.5×3.0 m) · Guest Room / Store (3.0×3.0 m) · Bathroom (Ground) (1.8×2.0 m) · Staircase Hall (2.0×3.0 m)
Common Mistake When drawing interior walls, ensure the wall end snaps exactly to the exterior wall face. Use Tab to cycle snap points. Walls that do not connect properly cause roof and ceiling generation errors later.

Placing Doors
Traditional Kashmiri Carved Doors — Main Entrance, Bedroom & Interior

6.1 Main Entrance — Double-Leaf Carved Door

The traditional Kashmiri main entrance is a carved double-leaf deodar door with intricate Islamic/floral geometric motifs. In Revit, we load a suitable family and modify it.

1
Load a Door Family Architecture → Door → Load Family (or Insert → Load Family). Navigate to Doors folder. Select Double-Flush.rfa as base.
2
Set Door Dimensions In Properties: Width = 1200 mm (double-leaf = 2 × 600 mm). Height = 2100 mm. Frame Width = 75 mm (deodar timber).
3
Apply Material — Deodar Wood Edit Type → Door Material → Browse → "Wood" → select Wood — Walnut. Apply warm brown color (RGB 101, 67, 33).
4
Place on South Exterior Wall Click on the south wall approximately at center (4.55 m from west corner). Verify swing direction — inward-opening for Kashmiri homes.
5
Add Sill & Frame Details Set Sill Height = 0 mm (flush with floor). Add a 200 mm stone threshold (Specialty Equipment family) below the door.

Placing Windows
Kashmiri Lattice Windows (Dab), Custom Families & Window Schedule

Windows in traditional Kashmiri homes are characterized by their wooden lattice screens (Dab), lower-than-standard sill heights, and carved deodar frames. For modern Kashmiri houses as shown on the channel, uPVC or aluminum frames with decorative grills are used.

7.1 Making a Simple Window in Revit (Beginner Method)

This mirrors the tutorial: "How to make a simple window in Revit — for beginners || Kashmir House Plans"

1
Architecture → Window Click the Window tool in the Architecture tab → Build group.
2
Select Window Type In the Type Selector, choose Fixed: 0610 × 0610 mm as a starting base. We will edit this.
3
Edit Type → Duplicate → Name: Kashmiri_Dab_Window_1200x1200 Set: Width = 1200 mm · Height = 1200 mm · Frame Width = 65 mm · Frame Material = Deodar Wood.
4
Set Sill Height in Instance Properties After placing, select the window. In Properties panel: Sill Height = 900 mm (standard for Kashmiri rooms). Head Height auto-calculates to 2100 mm.
5
Verify Arrow Direction The arrow symbol on the window shows exterior/interior faces. Ensure it points to the outside. If reversed, click the Flip icon that appears when the window is selected.
6
Place Windows on All Four Exterior Walls Follow the window schedule below. Place using exact offset dimensions from wall corners (use the Temporary Dimension that appears after placement).
7
Add Lattice Pattern (Dab) via Material For an authentic look, apply a custom material with a lattice texture image to the glass pane layer of the window family.

Chapter 08
Kashmiri Roof Design
Complex Pitched Roof — Hip + Gable Combination for Snow Load

Based on tutorials: "Complex roof tutorial in Revit || Kashmir house plans" and "Complex Kashmiri roof design || Kashmir house plans"

The Kashmiri roof is the most distinctive and technically challenging element to model in Revit. It is a steeply pitched hip-gable combination roof, designed to shed heavy Himalayan snowfall. The pitch ranges from 30° to 45°, with a generous eave overhang of 600–900 mm.

8.1 Creating the Roof by Footprint

1
Switch to Floor Plan — Roof Level Project Browser → Floor Plans → Roof Level.
2
Architecture → Roof → Roof by Footprint Click the Roof dropdown → Roof by Footprint. Revit enters sketch mode.
3
Use Pick Walls to Trace Exterior Walls In the Options Bar: check Defines Slope. Set Overhang = 700 mm. Click on each of the four exterior walls to create the roof boundary.
4
Set the Roof Pitch Select all four boundary lines. In Properties, set Slope = 35.00° (or 8:12 rise/run for imperial). This gives the characteristic steep Kashmiri pitch.
5
Create Gable Ends (Uncheck Slope on Two Sides) For a Gable roof: select the two shorter boundary lines → in Options Bar uncheck Defines Slope. These become vertical gable walls. For a Hip roof: leave all four sides with slope defined.
6
Finish the Roof Sketch Click the green checkmark (✓) to finish editing. Revit generates the 3D roof form.
7
Attach Walls to Roof Select all exterior walls of the upper floor. Modify → Modify Walls → Attach Top/Base. Click Attach Top, then click the roof. Walls will cut to follow the roof slope at gable ends.

8.3 Measuring Roof Dimensions in Revit

Based on: "How to measure roof dimensions in Revit || quick revit tutorial || Kashmir house plans"

1
Open the Roof in 3D View — Orbit to get a clear view of the roof surface.
2
Annotate → Aligned Dimension — Click two points on the roof edge to measure the rafter length (eave to ridge).
3
Use Temporary Dimensions — Click on any roof edge; temporary dimensions appear showing the slope length, width, and overhang automatically.
Common Revit Roof Error — "Too Many Intersections" If Revit shows a warning about roof intersections, it usually means boundary lines are not forming a clean closed loop. Zoom in at corners and ensure no overlaps or gaps exist between boundary sketch lines before finishing.

Chapter 09
Roof Fascia & Soffit
The Easiest Way — As Shown in Kashmir House Plans Tutorial

Based on: "Roof fascia and soffit Revit tutorial easiest way || Kashmir house plans"

9.1 Adding Fascia

1
Select the Roof → Architecture → Fascia Click the roof in 3D view. Go to Architecture tab → Roof group → Fascia (within the Roof dropdown).
2
Load a Fascia Profile Family Use Rectangular:75×200mm profile. Material: Deodar Wood (painted white or natural). This is the traditional Kashmiri fascia board.
3
Click Each Eave Edge Click on each of the four eave edges of the roof (at the 700 mm overhang). Revit places the fascia board along the entire eave length.

9.2 Adding Soffit

1
Architecture → Roof → Soffit The soffit fills the underside of the overhang — the horizontal surface between the wall face and the fascia.
2
Sketch the Soffit Boundary Draw a rectangle from the exterior wall face out to the fascia position (700 mm wide, all four sides). Material: Painted timber boarding.
3
Finish and Verify in 3D Click ✓. In 3D view, the eave soffit should now show as a clean horizontal panel below the roof overhang.
Kashmiri Soffit Tradition In authentic Kashmiri homes, the soffit is made from deodar planks (25 mm thick, 100 mm wide) laid parallel to the eave, painted white or left natural. This is called Tul locally and protects the eave structure from rain. Khatamband-style geometric patterns are sometimes applied to the soffit of verandahs.
Chapter 10
Zoon Dub — Cantilevered Balcony
Kashmir's Iconic Moon-Viewing Overhanging Balcony

The Zoon Dub (meaning "Moon Balcony") is a defining feature of Srinagar's urban residential architecture. It is a cantilevered balcony projecting 1.0–1.5 m from the first floor facade, enclosed with intricate wooden lattice screens (Pinjarakari) for privacy and decorative effect.

10.1 Creating the Zoon Dub in Revit

1
Floor Plan — Level 2. Architecture → Floor Create a new floor extending 1.2 m beyond the south exterior wall on the first floor. This is the cantilevered slab.
2
Sketch the Balcony Slab Draw the floor boundary: 3.6 m wide × 1.2 m deep, centered on south facade. Floor type: RCC_Slab_125mm. Finish (✓).
3
Add Balcony Enclosure Walls Draw three short walls (two side walls 1.2 m long, one front wall 3.6 m long) at 1.0 m height around the balcony perimeter. Type: Kashmiri_Int_Wall_115mm.
4
Add Lattice Railing (Pinjarakari) Architecture → Railing → Sketch Path. Trace the three balcony edges. Select railing type with a lattice panel infill. Material: Walnut wood, dark finish.
5
Add Access Door D9 Place door D9 (900×2200 mm, glass + deodar frame) on the south wall at Level 2, opening into the Zoon Dub. This is typically in the master bedroom.
6
Add Roof / Canopy Over Zoon Dub Create a small projected roof over the Zoon Dub using Roof by Footprint, pitch 30°, overhang 300 mm on three sides.

Chapter 11
Khatamband Ceiling
Kashmir's Signature Geometric Interlocking Wood-Panel Ceiling

The Khatamband is one of Kashmir's greatest craft traditions — a ceiling composed of small interlocking pieces of deodar, walnut, or fir wood fitted together in geometric patterns without nails or glue. The joinery alone holds the structure. Patterns include Muraba (square), Kansut, Sarva, Lakut, Badam (almond), and Sakhur.

11.1 Creating a Khatamband Ceiling in Revit

1
Architecture → Ceiling → Compound Ceiling In Floor Plan Level 1 view, click the Ceiling tool. Select Compound Ceiling: 600×600 mm Grid as base type.
2
Create Ceiling Type: Khatamband_Deodar Edit Type → Duplicate. In Structure layers: Deodar Wood panels 20 mm. Set ceiling height = 2700 mm above floor (300 mm below the 3000 mm floor-to-floor).
3
Apply Wood Material with Geometric Pattern Material: Wood — Walnut/Deodar. In the material's Surface Pattern, apply a geometric hatch pattern that mimics the Khatamband grid. Use a custom 45° diagonal diamond pattern.
4
Click Inside Room Boundaries Revit auto-detects room boundaries. Click inside the Living Room, Master Bedroom, and Dining Room to place the Khatamband ceiling. Bathrooms and kitchen get a plain plaster ceiling.
5
Verify Height in Section View Cut a section through the living room. The ceiling should sit at 2700 mm, leaving a 300 mm plenum space above for concealed wiring and the structural slab.
Khatamband Pattern Reference For photorealistic Lumion rendering, use a high-resolution Khatamband texture photograph as the material bitmap. Search for "khatamband ceiling texture" and apply it as a Decal or Surface Pattern with a scale of 300×300 mm per panel unit for accurate proportions.

Chapter 13
Lumion Rendering
Photorealistic Kashmiri House Visualization — As Used on the Channel

Based on: "Designing a Modern 35×35 House with Revit and Lumion || Kashmir House Plans" and "Kashmiri House Design rendering in Revit New (tutorial)"

Lumion is the primary rendering software used on the Kashmir House Plans channel for producing photorealistic exterior and interior visualizations. The workflow moves from Revit → Lumion via the LiveSync plugin or direct export.

13.1 Exporting from Revit to Lumion

1
Install Lumion LiveSync Plugin for Revit Download from lumion.com/revit-rendering. Install. Restart Revit. A new "Lumion" tab appears in the ribbon.
2
Open LiveSync Lumion tab → LiveSync → Start LiveSync. Open Lumion separately. The model appears live in Lumion and updates as you make changes in Revit.
3
Alternatively: Export as Collada (.dae) File → Export → CAD Formats → Collada (.dae). Open Lumion → Import → Browse to the .dae file. Scale: 1:1, Units: Meters.

A.1 Kashmiri Architectural Glossary

Taq (تاق)

Traditional load-bearing masonry construction system using stone piers (taq = "arch" or "opening") connected by horizontal deodar timber bands at each floor level. The openings between piers become windows and doors.

Dhajji Dewari (ڈھاجی دیواری)

Braced timber frame construction with brick or stone masonry infill panels. The timber grid creates a "patchwork quilt" pattern on the exterior wall. Highly earthquake-resistant — outperformed modern construction in the 2005 Kashmir earthquake.

Zoon Dub (زون دب)

Literally "Moon Platform" — a cantilevered balcony projecting from the first floor, used traditionally for moon-gazing and social interaction. Characteristically enclosed with wooden lattice (Pinjarakari) screens. The most iconic element of Srinagar's traditional residential architecture.

Khatamband (خاتم بند)

Art of creating ceilings using interlocking small pieces of deodar, walnut, or fir wood in geometric patterns. Introduced in Kashmir in 1541 by Mirza Hyder Doulat. No nails, no glue — pure joinery. Patterns include Muraba, Kansut, Badam, Sarva, Bod, Lakut, Sakhur, Tobul.

Pinjarakari (پنجرکاری)

Intricately carved wooden lattice fretwork used as screens on balconies, windows, and facades. Both functional (privacy, ventilation) and decorative. Often features Islamic geometric patterns or floral Mughal motifs.

Dab / Kandhak

Traditional Kashmiri wooden lattice window screen inserted within window openings. The lattice filters light and provides privacy. Made from deodar or walnut in geometric patterns.

Hamam (حمام)

A traditional underfloor heating system where a sunken chamber beneath the floor is heated by burning wood or charcoal coals. The warm floor radiates heat upward. Found in traditional sitting rooms (Dewan Khana).

Dewan Khana

The principal reception room or formal sitting room in a traditional Kashmiri home, typically featuring Khatamband ceiling, Pinjarakari screens, Hamam floor heating, and the most elaborate woodwork in the house.
